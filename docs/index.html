Fear & Greed
In plain English:
This is basically a mood ring for crypto traders. It measures whether people are panicking or getting greedy right now — nothing more mystical than that.
0–24 Extreme Fear — everyone's panic selling
25–44 Fear — people are nervous
45–55 Neutral — nobody's sure
56–75 Greed — people are getting excited
76–100 Extreme Greed — euphoria (often a warning, not a green light)
It's not a crystal ball. It just tells you the crowd's emotional temperature — what people do with that is up to them.
Data from Alternative.me's Crypto Fear & Greed Index — running since 2018 and cited by outlets like CoinDesk and Bloomberg. We didn't make this number up.

⭐ Top Story

🤔 WTF Does This Mean?

Days after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission submitted its own draft crypto rules to the White House's Office of Management and Budget for review — a required step before a federal agency can formally propose a new rule. The filing is light on public detail: it's not yet clear which crypto assets it would cover or what exchanges would need to do to qualify. Once OMB finishes reviewing, it goes back to the CFTC for a vote, then out for public comment, then a final vote before anything takes effect.

💡 Why It Matters

With Congress stuck, this is the CFTC trying to build a rulebook using authority it already has, rather than waiting on a law that may not come for years. Paired with the SEC's tokenized-stock exemption from the day before, it's a signal that US regulators are trying to fill the gap Congress left rather than sit on their hands — even though nothing changes for traders yet.
